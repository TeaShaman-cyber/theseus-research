# Theseus 1.0 Migration Map — From Public Proposal to Active Research Program

**Date:** 2026-09-06
**Status:** candidate migration map / review artifact
**Authority:** non-normative; this document does not itself change the Theseus contract, methodology, registry, or research conclusions
**Observed baseline:** `TeaShaman-cyber/theseus-research@0a2a908985e7e96b014a085da911db32926a6c38` plus the open PR/Issue states pinned below

## 1. Purpose

Bring the root `theseus-research` repository into alignment with the program that actually exists now.

The repository began as a small public contract proposed for review. Theseus is now an active, maintainer-supported research program with multiple research lines, working laboratories, a growing evidence corpus, versioned design work, GitHub-based coordination, CI, and a candidate machine-readable research-line registry.

The migration must update the public model without collapsing distinct authority layers or pretending that unfinished research has become accepted methodology.

This map therefore separates four operations that are easy to confuse:

```text
repository cleanup
!= research merge
!= methodology promotion
!= contract revision
```

Each operation gets its own review and observable postcondition.

## 2. Feynman explanation

Imagine Theseus as a workshop that opened with one sign on the door:

> "A small research proposal is here for public review."

While that sign remained unchanged, the workshop acquired several benches, experiments, tools, notebooks, safety rules, and a registry showing which benches belong to the workshop.

The problem is not that the new work is wrong. The sign is wrong because it still describes the workshop as if nobody had started working inside it.

The repair is therefore not "put every experiment on the sign." The repair is:

```text
front door       -> what Theseus is and what governs it
methodology      -> how accepted work is performed
registry         -> which research lines belong to Theseus
research library -> durable evidence and synthesis
Issues / PRs     -> what is being investigated or changed now
changelog        -> which contract revisions were actually accepted
```

A new reader should be able to answer "what is this?", "what belongs to it?", "what is accepted?", and "what is still research?" without reading repository archaeology.

## 3. Five Whys

### Why 1 — Why is the root repository misleading now?

Because `README.md` still says `0.3-draft`, `proposed for public review`, describes an intentionally small first version, and says the repository contains the program-level contract only.

### Why 2 — Why did the README become stale?

Because subsequent development happened through research notes, Issues, PRs, laboratories, CI, and cross-project operational work while the root lifecycle description remained unchanged.

### Why 3 — Why does the research-line registry not solve this automatically?

Because registry membership answers one narrow question:

```text
Which research lines are explicitly part of Theseus?
```

It must not become authority for research conclusions, live engineering status, methodology, or the program contract.

### Why 4 — Why can methodology not simply absorb the new work?

Because methodology should contain accepted reusable practice. Research artifacts also contain hypotheses, failed routes, degraded evidence, candidate designs, and unresolved questions. Merging a research PR must not silently promote all of that into methodology.

### Why 5 — Why is this mismatch becoming costly now?

Because Theseus crossed a lifecycle boundary:

```text
public proposal
    -> active research program
```

The information architecture still reflects the proposal phase, so humans and agents must reconstruct the real program from scattered state. That increases navigation cost, authority confusion, stale claims, and accidental promotion.

## 4. Target program identity

Recommended lifecycle transition:

```text
0.3-draft / proposed for public review
    -> 1.0 / active research program
```

`1.0` does **not** mean research is complete. It means the public contract and repository structure describe an already operating program rather than a proposal waiting to begin.

Candidate status block for the eventual bilingual contract revision:

```text
Version: 1.0
Program status: ACTIVE RESEARCH
Operating model: independent, voluntary, maintainer-supported public-interest research
Review model: open continuous review; ordinary research work does not depend on receiving external review
```

The exact 1.0 wording remains subject to a separate bilingual contract diff and explicit maintainer acceptance.

## 5. Funding and support model

Theseus exists because the work is useful to its maintainer and collaborators, not because an external sponsor commissioned it.

Current operating posture:

```text
practical need
    -> voluntary / maintainer-supported work
    -> public research where appropriate
    -> compatible outside help may accelerate the work
```

External help, compute, grants, donations, sponsorship, or other funding are not categorically rejected. They are compatible only when they do not override the program's ethical purpose or buy research authority.

Candidate principle:

> Support may fund or accelerate the work; it must not purchase the right to determine findings, conceal negative results, weaken consent or safety boundaries, force advertising, or override the program's ethical purpose and independent research judgment.

This preserves the existing sponsor-independence invariant while accurately describing a project maintained primarily from practical need and voluntary effort.

The funding mechanism is an operating fact, not a permanent requirement: future compatible support does not make Theseus less independent merely because money or compute is involved.

## 6. Target repository information architecture

```text
theseus-research/
│
├── README.md
├── README.ru.md
│   program contract + front door + current program identity
│
├── CHANGELOG.md
│   accepted contract revision history
│
├── docs/
│   ├── methodology.md
│   ├── methodology.ru.md
│   │   accepted working methodology
│   │
│   ├── research/
│   │   ├── README.md
│   │   │   navigation for durable research artifacts
│   │   └── ...
│   │       research notes, syntheses, fixtures, receipts where appropriate
│   │
│   ├── designs/          # candidate architecture/design artifacts, if/when public path migration is accepted
│   └── plans/            # execution plans/provenance, if worth retaining publicly
│
├── registry/
│   └── research-lines.json
│       declared Theseus research-line membership and metadata contract
│
├── tools/ + tests/ + .github/
│   registry validation/projection tooling and CI
│
└── GitHub Issues / PRs
    live research, engineering, review, and coordination state
```

### Boundary rules

- `README*` defines the program contract and provides navigation; it is not a live task tracker.
- `CHANGELOG.md` records accepted contract revisions; it does not record every research merge.
- `docs/methodology*` contains promoted reusable practice; research merge alone cannot change this authority.
- `registry/research-lines.json` declares membership; GitHub metadata and README tables are projections.
- `docs/research/` stores durable public evidence/synthesis; a research note remains evidence, not authority.
- candidate designs remain candidate designs even when preserved in Git history.
- Issues/PRs remain the authoritative live state for their own research/change lifecycle.

## 7. Navigation model

The root README should answer only the first routing question and point downward:

```text
What is Theseus and what governs it?
    -> README / README.ru

How is accepted work performed?
    -> docs/methodology.*

Which research lines belong to Theseus?
    -> registry/research-lines.json
       + generated README EN/RU projection

What durable research has been published here?
    -> docs/research/README.md

What is being investigated right now?
    -> GitHub Issues

What candidate change or design is under review?
    -> GitHub PRs

Which contract revisions were accepted?
    -> CHANGELOG.md
```

`docs/research/README.md` should be a small navigation index, **not a second registry** and not a manually maintained copy of all open PRs. It may classify durable artifacts by type/status and point readers to Issues/PRs for live state.

## 9. Migration sequence

These phases are migration tracks, not a single blocking pipeline unless a dependency is stated explicitly. In particular, registry hardening in Phase A does **not** gate the lifecycle correction in Phase E.

### Phase A — Harden the registry as an independent infrastructure track

**Goal:** make the research-line registry and its projections safe enough to become baseline infrastructure without making registry completion a prerequisite for an accurate 1.0 lifecycle contract.

1. Re-probe PR #5 exact head and unresolved threads.
2. Address the currently observed registry findings:
   - private repository names must never enter public candidate-discovery reports;
   - incomplete GitHub search results must classify discovery as `UNREACHABLE`, not `PASS`;
   - drift-issue write/readback failure must still emit a fail-closed JSON report;
   - EN/RU README projection writes must remain atomic across write failures.
3. Add repository hygiene for generated Python bytecode so local verification leaves a clean worktree.
4. Re-run the complete registry suite, schema validation, render check, doctor checks, and exact-head review.
5. Verify generated README EN/RU registry projections against `registry/research-lines.json`.
6. Human reviews and explicitly merges PR #5 only after exact remote postconditions are clean.
7. Read back merged commit, `CHANGELOG.md`, registry JSON, and both README projections.

**Postcondition:** when this track is merged, `main` has a named acceptance authority, machine-readable research-line registry, and bilingual registry projections that agree with one another. If this track remains blocked, Phase E may still correct the program lifecycle without claiming the candidate registry as accepted baseline.

### Phase B — Preserve reviewed research evidence without promoting it

**Goal:** bring durable evidence into `main` while keeping research and methodology separate.

1. Re-probe PR #14 exact head and review state.
2. If exact-head review remains clean, merge PR #14 as research/synthesis evidence.
3. Read back the merged research note from `main`.
4. Do **not** modify methodology merely because PR #14 merged.

**Postcondition:** harness-orientation and configured-project canary evidence are durable public research, with degraded/unknown claims still labeled as such.

### Phase C — Make the research forest navigable

**Goal:** stop using the root README as a hand-maintained list of individual research notes.

1. Add `docs/research/README.md` as a small durable research-library index.
2. Classify only durable repository artifacts, for example:
   - empirical/evaluation note;
   - synthesis/architecture lineage;
   - fixture/receipt;
   - candidate design location.
3. Explain that current research state lives in GitHub Issues/PRs rather than duplicating it in the index.
4. Update `README.md` and `README.ru.md` navigation to point to:
   - methodology;
   - research-line registry;
   - research library;
   - Issues/PRs for live work;
   - changelog for accepted contract history.
5. Avoid adding a second machine-readable research registry.

**Postcondition:** a new reader can navigate the program without reconstructing its structure from branch names or old chat history.

### Phase D — Distill methodology from reviewed research

**Goal:** promote only reusable principles that survived evidence/review, not whole research artifacts.

Use PR #13 and already merged research as candidate sources. Candidate principles currently worth a separate bilingual methodology review include:

```text
local green != system/forest green

evidence != authority

a successful fallback != proof of route equivalence

degraded evidence remains degraded until the missing equivalence/postcondition is verified

authority/provenance used for binding decisions must be reconstructible
```

Do **not** promote claims that remain observational or unresolved, including:

```text
thin-router-v6 causally guarantees project boot routing
Sleuth is a verified fallback
Project Instructions reliably select the intended harness in every fresh chat
```

Required process:

1. Prepare one bounded EN/RU methodology diff.
2. Cite the exact research artifacts supporting each promoted principle.
3. Verify EN/RU semantic parity.
4. Human explicitly accepts the methodology change.
5. Keep PR #13's design merge/provenance decision distinct from this promotion.

**Postcondition:** methodology contains stable reusable practice and remains smaller than the research forest that produced it.

### Phase E — Publish the active-program 1.0 contract revision

**Goal:** make the root contract describe the program that actually exists.

Prepare one bilingual `README.md` / `README.ru.md` contract revision with an accompanying `CHANGELOG.md` entry.

Recommended semantic changes:

1. `0.3-draft` -> candidate `1.0`.
2. `proposed for public review` -> `ACTIVE RESEARCH`.
3. Replace "intentionally small first version" lifecycle wording with continuous public review wording.
4. State that external review is welcome and informative but is not a gate for ordinary research work.
5. Describe the repository as program contract + methodology + public research archive/coordination root, not "program-level contract only"; claim a machine-readable research-line map as accepted baseline only if the registry track has actually merged.
6. Record the voluntary / maintainer-supported operating posture.
7. State that compatible outside help/funding is welcome when it does not override ethical purpose, independent findings, consent/safety boundaries, provenance, or publication integrity.
8. Preserve non-commercial public-interest status unless a separate reviewed contract change explicitly changes it.
9. Preserve the rule that external support cannot control findings or conceal negative results.
10. Keep research-line membership in the registry track rather than hard-coding an independent competing authority list; if that track is still pending, describe it as pending rather than blocking the lifecycle correction.
11. Include the candidate `1.0` `CHANGELOG.md` entry in the same bilingual contract PR. Review README EN/RU and the changelog entry together; the maintainer merge makes the contract revision and its changelog record accepted atomically.

**Postcondition:** the public contract no longer describes Theseus as waiting to begin, while preserving the ethical and authority boundaries that matter.

Open branches currently use `docs/superpowers/specs/` and `docs/superpowers/plans/` for design provenance. That path reflects an authoring workflow/plugin rather than a public document type.

Recommended 1.0 cleanup policy:

```text
docs/superpowers/specs/*  -> docs/designs/*
docs/superpowers/plans/*  -> docs/plans/*
```

Do this only when the affected artifacts are about to enter `main`, and update all known repository links in the same reviewed change. Do not mass-move open branch history merely for cosmetic consistency.

## 11. Authority and acceptance gates

The migration must preserve these separations:

```text
public review informs
maintainer explicitly accepts contract/methodology changes
merge proves repository acceptance only for the merged artifact
research evidence does not grant authority
registry membership does not validate a research result
GitHub UI metadata is a projection, not canonical research truth
funding/support does not grant research or publication authority
```

Every stateful step should use exact remote readback appropriate to the domain.

### Contract acceptance

A contract revision becomes accepted only after:

```text
bilingual diff
-> review
-> explicit maintainer merge
-> CHANGELOG entry in the merged state
-> exact remote readback
```

### Methodology acceptance

Methodology promotion is a separate explicit EN/RU change and must not be inferred from merging its source research.

### Research merge

A research merge means only that the research artifact and its evidence boundary are preserved in the accepted repository history.

## 12. What does not block 1.0

The following are explicitly **not** prerequisites for the active-program transition:

- finishing all open research Issues;
- implementing adaptive route weighting;
- proving a universal ChatGPT boot mechanism;
- validating Sleuth as a fallback;
- completing every cookbook/Skill design;
- finding external reviewers;
- receiving grants, sponsorship, or outside funding;
- turning every private experiment into a public repository;
- creating a centralized dashboard or GitHub Project as new authority.

Requiring these would recreate the same mistake under a new name: making truthful program status wait on unrelated research completion.

## 13. 1.0 readiness checklist

A candidate 1.0 contract revision is ready for maintainer acceptance when all of the following are observable:

- [ ] the 1.0 contract does not claim the candidate registry is accepted unless the registry track has actually merged.
- [ ] root README no longer claims the repository contains only the program contract.
- [ ] root README no longer claims ordinary program work is merely proposed and waiting for public review.
- [ ] continuous public review is preserved without becoming an execution gate.
- [ ] maintainer-supported / voluntary operating posture is described accurately.
- [ ] compatible external support is allowed without weakening sponsor-independence and ethical-purpose boundaries.
- [ ] durable research navigation exists without creating a second registry.
- [ ] promoted methodology changes, if included in the 1.0 migration, were reviewed separately in EN/RU.
- [ ] candidate/unresolved research remains visibly candidate/unresolved.
- [ ] the candidate 1.0 `CHANGELOG.md` entry is reviewed in the same diff as README EN/RU and becomes accepted atomically with maintainer merge.
- [ ] final remote main SHA and critical files are read back after merge.

## 14. Migration stop conditions

Stop and classify rather than forcing progress when:

- PR #5 validation cannot protect private-incubation identities;
- EN/RU contract or methodology meaning diverges;
- a research result is being promoted only because its PR is convenient to merge;
- an external support condition would influence findings, conceal negative evidence, weaken ethical boundaries, or create hidden publication authority;
- a fallback path is treated as equivalent without evidence;
- the proposed 1.0 wording weakens an existing invariant under the guise of lifecycle cleanup;
- a navigation mechanism becomes a second source of authority.

## 15. Desired end state

Feynman version:

> Theseus is an active voluntary public-interest research program maintained because its work is practically useful. It has stable ethical rules, an explicit way to accept changes, a map of its research lines, a library for durable evidence, and live spaces for unfinished work. Outside review and compatible support are welcome, but neither is required for the program to continue and neither buys authority over its findings or purpose.

Operationally:

```text
truthful front door
+ explicit accepted methodology
+ machine-readable research-line membership
+ navigable research evidence
+ live Issues/PRs for unfinished work
+ reviewable changelog
+ independent ethical/support boundary
= Theseus 1.0 candidate baseline
```
